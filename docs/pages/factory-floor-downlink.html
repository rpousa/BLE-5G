Factory Floor Downlink

One factory floor, three downlinks from the edge (ext-dn) at the same time. A LiDAR on a pole in the middle of the floor streams its scans to nrUE1 (eMBB). The positioning server sends where each of the four AGVs is to nrUE2 (URLLC). Two ceiling cameras send JPEG video to n3iwue1 and n3iwue2 over Bluetooth. Every scan, position and frame below is the one that actually arrived, at the time it arrived. Pick a load level and watch what each UE knows about the floor.

What the run shows

  1. 5G NR downlink. LiDAR and robot positions share one gNB radio. At level 1 and 2 (22 and 45 kbit/s together) every frame arrives: LiDAR scans in 230–280 ms (median), robot updates in 110–145 ms. Level 3 asks for 95 kbit/s, just above the 90 kbit/s the path carried cleanly on 5 Nov. Nothing is lost, but the queue grows to 17 s for LiDAR and 6 s for the robots. The backlog does not drain in the 10 s gap, so level 4 starts 22 s behind and then drops 57 % of the scans and 31 % of the positions.
  2. For the robots, the delay is the error. An AGV at 1.4 m/s moves 15–20 cm in the 110–145 ms an update takes at levels 1 and 2. At level 3 the controller sees each AGV where it was seconds ago, metres off. The floor map shows that gap live.
  3. Bluetooth video. Level 1 (8 kbit/s per camera) is clean: every frame in 380–440 ms, mostly the time a 2 KB frame needs at the 40 kbit/s link pacing. At level 2 (16.5 kbit/s) a third of the frames are lost and the rest take 5–6 s, even though both links stay under their pacing: they share the core's one Intel adapter, and IPsec and GRE add about 10 % per packet. At levels 3 and 4 almost nothing gets through.
  4. No board hung. Downlink-only load never hung a BLE board (watchdog log: no HCI timeouts), which fits the firmware finding that only PC→board traffic hangs hci_bb. n3iwue1 did lose its session under the overload: its IKE and NAS share the starved link. The watchdog restarted it at the end of level 2 (dead for all of level 3) and again in level 4, so CAM1 shows nothing from level 3 on.
  5. How the delay is measured. The sender in ext-dn stamps each packet with the core PC's clock. The UE PC stamps its arrival. The two clocks differ by 8 h 48 min, so a LAN exchange (best of 100, 0.4 ms round trip) measured the offset before and after the run. It drifted 1.7 ms in 5 minutes, which is the measurement's error bar. A frame's delay runs from its first packet leaving to its last packet arriving.
