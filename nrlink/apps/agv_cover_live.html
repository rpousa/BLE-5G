AGV Dual Gateway

Three AGVs and one gNB. An AGV the gNB cannot reach sends through another AGV over the sidelink: a 3GPP layer-3 relay, on gNB-scheduled sidelink. More in the Annex ›

Three AGVs, one gNB on a pole. The two AGVs nearest the pole are both connected to the gNB: each is a gateway with its own UE and PDU session, and sends its own data straight up. The third, the remote, is one sidelink hop from either gateway and chooses, for each message, which one to send through. A variant of the AGV relay chain, run on the same four boards. The live scenario is now coverage: an AGV in the shielded cold store reaches the edge through another AGV acting as a layer-3 UE-to-network relay, as in 3GPP TS 23.304: it discovers the relay, sets up a PC5 direct link, gets an IP address from it, and its IP packets are routed and NATed into the relay's PDU session. Since 20 Nov the gNB also schedules the sidelink (mode 1): each AGV with an NR session gets its own slice of the window, the remote a shared pool.

    Packet sequence and slot scheme

    a remote positioning message and a command, through a layer-3 relay · how NR and the sidelink share the air

    Layer-3 relay (TS 23.304): discovery, a PC5 link, an IP address from the relay, then routing and NAT into its PDU session. More in the Annex ›

    As a layer-3 UE-to-network relay in TS 23.304, with the procedures of TS 24.554 and the thresholds of TS 38.304 / 38.331. A: the thresholds are configured as SIB12 would carry them (field names of SL-RelayUE-Config, SL-RemoteUE-Config, SL-ReselectionConfig). An AGV whose Uu RSRP is between threshLowRelay (−95 dBm) and threshHighRelay (−31 dBm), whose session has settled and whose relay PDU session (DNN relay, its second session) is up, sends a Model A discovery announcement with the relay service code on each PC5 link every 0.5 s. An AGV whose Uu falls below threshHighRemote (−100 dBm) picks the strongest announcement above sl-RSRP-Thresh (−90 dBm), keeping its relay unless another is 2 dB stronger, and sets up the link with PC5-S: request, security mode command and complete (null algorithms), accept with "IP address configuration: DHCPv4 server". It then asks the relay's DHCPv4 server for an address from that relay's pool, and the relay sends a Remote UE Report with it up the relay PDU session. When the remote has its own session again it releases the address and the link (DHCPRELEASE, DIRECT LINK RELEASE REQUEST / ACCEPT), and the relay reports that too. B: the remote's app sends ordinary UDP to the edge. Inside the relay AGV a policy route sends what arrives on the sidelink through a GRE tunnel to the nrUE's namespace, which masquerades it into the relay PDU session (12.1.2.0/24), apart from the relay's own traffic (12.1.1.0/24). Since then: the security mode command picks real algorithms, the keys come from both sides' nonces (after TS 33.503) and every PC5-S message from it on carries a MAC; the remote's traffic and DHCP go in ESP (AES-GCM) over the sidelink, its DSCP copied to the ESP header, so each flow keeps its QoS class (positioning PQI 58 → 5QI 7, LiDAR PQI 59 → 5QI 9) to the relay session's priority queue; the SD-RSRP adds each link's measured deviation to the path-loss model; and the Remote UE Report goes as NAS to the SMF, which answers it (a copy still goes to the edge for this page). Emulated: the PC5 discovery and PC5-S messages and DHCP go as UDP over the sidelink IP (OAI's sidelink carries only IP); the PC5-S information elements are tag-length-value; the PC5 root key is generated per run, not provisioned; ESP stands in for PDCP ciphering on PC5; the distance part of the SD-RSRP is modelled, since the boards share a desk. C, since 20 Nov: the sidelink is scheduled by the gNB (mode 1, TS 38.321). The relay, and every other AGV with its own NR session, reports its sidelink buffer in its uplink; the gNB gives each a slice of the next window sized to it, and the window ends with a pool for AGVs out of coverage (mode 2), which is where the remote sends. The boards (nrlink v7) hold the staged packets and send them only in their slice. See the Sidelink mode 1 tab.

    Mode 1 (TS 38.321): the gNB gives each AGV a slice of the window from its buffer report, and a pool to an AGV out of coverage. More in the Annex ›

    Mode 1 as in TS 38.321 (5.22.1.6), on the boards: each AGV whose UE has an NR session adds a sidelink buffer status report (bytes and packets waiting) to its uplink. After slot 9 the gNB bridge turns the reports into grants: one slice of the window per AGV, sized to its report (1 Mbit/s plus the per-packet cost), 0.8 ms apart, the first one 1 ms after the announcement, the order rotating each window; then a pool for AGVs out of coverage (mode 2), 4 ms, kept at 8 ms or more while a relay reports that it carries one, and grown when the gNB board hears it busy (CBR above 0.45). The announcement carries the grants; each board (nrlink v7) holds the packets the controller staged on it and sends them only in its own slice, timed in microseconds from the announcement, then reports what it sent. The window ends with the last slice, so a quiet frame gives the air back to NR. Over the run, 551 windows were full (more asked than 25 ms holds, every grant shrunk alike) and 6835 had a remote UE behind a relay. 30 sidelink messages arrived cut short and were dropped before reaching a UE (see findings).

    Fixed window: after slot 9 of each frame the gNB stays off the air for 25 ms of sidelink. More in the Annex ›

    Fixed window
    (the slot scheme tab), air sharing as in the relay chain: after slot 9 of every TDD frame the gNB bridge announces a 25 ms sidelink window and stays off the air; the boards carry the sidelink UEs' slots (PSBCH, SCI, transport blocks) only inside it, beacons first. The rest of the frame the boards carry the gNB's slots: downlink to every attached UE, and polls that each attached UE's board answers with its uplink. The gNB board's LED shows this split: a green flash for each NR stretch, a blue one for each window. With the layer-3 relay nothing changes on the air: the PC5 signalling and the remote's IP packets are transport blocks of the sidelink UEs like any other, and the relay's uplink, its own and its remote's, goes in its own UE's grants. An AGV on its own UE skips the sidelink entirely.

    Two gateways carried L2 with at most 3.4 % lost; the relay chain lost up to 57 %. More in the Annex ›

    • Two gateways carry L2 where the chain could not. With the usual 25 ms windows every flow lost 3.4 % or less (the chain: 9 % at the pivot, 38 % at the relay, 57 % at the remote), and a command reached the remote in 134 ms with 1.6 % lost (the chain: 458 ms, 51 %). Each sidelink hop lost 0.8 % or less.
    • Why: far less sidelink traffic. The middle AGV no longer hops at all, and the remote hops once instead of twice. At L2 the boards carried about 14 hops a second and used 15 ms of each 25 ms window; the chain needed more than its windows held (21 ms in use, and the rest aged out). The sidelink items left room for the position beacons too.
    • The uplink is now the limit. The two UEs together sent about 7.0 kB/s (56 kbit/s) up, close to the 62–65 kbit/s these boards carry on Uu. That is where the 0.5–0.9 s positioning delays at L2 come from: messages wait in the UEs for grants, but they are not lost.
    • Wider windows make it worse. At 35 and 50 ms the gNB runs fewer frames (8.5 and 7.8 a second instead of 9.8), so fewer uplink slots, and the uplink no longer keeps up: positioning messages waited 5–9 s and 4–38 % aged out, while the sidelink, now with room to spare, lost almost nothing. For two gateways the narrow window is right; the chain needed wider windows only because of its double hops.
    • The remote's choice matters. Sending all its traffic through gateway 1 (at 25 ms) made the positioning messages wait 2.5–3.4 s (p50) and the remote lose 6–7 %: gateway 1's UE queued while gateway 2's had room. Picking the gateway whose last beacon said it sent less split the remote's traffic about evenly and kept delays at 0.5–0.9 s. At 35 ms, where the uplink is overloaded either way, always-gateway-1 was still worse (15–22 s against 5–7 s).
    • L1: every flow lost 0.9 % or less. The remote's positioning took 205 ms (the chain: 330 ms over two hops), and gateway 2's own positioning 157 ms (the chain's relay: 215 ms).
    • About a third of the remote's messages went up its own UE. After a role change the old gateway, now the remote, keeps its session until the new gateway's UE has attached and settled (about 5 s plus 8 s), and while it has one it sends directly. With 3–4 role changes a minute that is a third of the time.
    • What is simulated is as on the relay chain page: the AGVs' motion, the application data, coverage (a UE out of coverage is stopped), and the PHY (GFSK packets carry the slots' contents). The gNB, the UEs, the sidelink stack, the core and every delay and loss here are real. The gateways' beacons are the controller's own packets; what the remote reads from them is the gateway's session state and the bytes it sent since its last beacon.
    • Live on the testbed. From 12 to 13 Nov the dual gateway was the testbed's live scenario (nrlink.sh live start --scenario dual on both PCs, the core PC first): L1, 25 ms windows, the remote on the sticky rule. The boards' LEDs show who carries what: red the remote, blue the gateway the remote sends through (NR and sidelink), green the other gateway (NR only). The core PC's live page (port 8090, on the LAN) shows this page with a live card on top: each board with the colour its LED shows, the AGVs moving in the same colours with both gateways' Uu links and the remote's sidelink to the blue gateway, every message's delay and the last minute's table. The sticky rule has not been through the L2 window tests; the recorded runs used the load rule.
    • Coverage by position (13 Nov, the live scenario). With the gNB's signal set by where each AGV is, every AGV lost its connection in the cold store once a lap: 18 radio link failures in 13 minutes, each followed by an attach that worked (median 3.7 s), and no message dropped for want of a session. The sidelink carried 26 % of all uplink messages, 25–26 % for each AGV. Over it, positioning took 189 ms at p50 against 128–140 ms on an AGV's own UE, LiDAR 325 against 220 ms, commands 90 against 63–66 ms, with at most 0.2 % lost. In that run the sticky pick moved the AGV between the two gateways every 5 s, because the one carrying it always looked busier; since then the live scenario picks the nearest gateway (keeping the current one while it is within 2 m of the nearest), which changes about twice per outage: once at the start and about once as the AGVs move.
    • Platoon, relays in reach, holding before the dead zone (13 Nov, briefly the live scenario). The AGVs drive as a convoy 8 m apart, and an AGV out of coverage relays only through the nearest AGV with a session that is nearer to it than the gNB. When only one AGV still has a settled session it stops 1.5 m short of the cold store until another has one again: 6 holds of 20 s in 13 minutes, and never a moment with all three AGVs cut off. 21 attaches, all up (median 3.5 s). The first try without a queue lost 2.2 % of the sidelink messages: the leader leaves the store while its UE is still attaching, and by then the held AGV is farther from it than the gNB, so it has no relay. With the app queuing for up to 5 s, 1 uplink message of 14,140 was lost and 49 arrived after more than 1 s. Over the sidelink: positioning 187 ms p50, LiDAR 308 ms, commands 75 ms; on an AGV's own UE 123–145, 220 and 62–66 ms.
    • No lost packets: one sidelink at a time, the platoon kept together (13 Nov, the live scenario until 16 Nov). An AGV with a session stops at the cold store's door only while another AGV is on the sidelink (as its relay, or because it would need a second sidelink); the AGV on the sidelink drives on to its own direct connection; and an AGV with a session waits for the one behind it, so the convoy stays within reach. In 14 minutes on the boards: 0 of 15,249 uplink messages lost, never two AGVs off the gNB, 11 holds (median 25 s), 2 % of messages over 1 s. Without keeping the convoy together the same rule lost 12 %, as the AGVs spread around the loop out of sidelink reach. The comparison card has every step.
    • The sidelink is kept until the NR link is up (16 Nov, the live scenario until 19 Nov). Leaving the cold store, AGV A (the leader) used to drive out of its relay's reach before its own UE had attached; the simulation puts that at 24–48 s of every 30 minutes with neither a session nor a relay, covered only by the 5 s uplink queue. Now an AGV on the sidelink keeps its relay until its own session is up, and stops before it would drive out of that relay's reach. On the boards: 6 stops in 14 minutes, all AGV A, about 3 s each; 0 uplink messages lost and none queued; sidelink positioning 180 ms p50, 287 ms p90.
    • A layer-3 relay, as in 3GPP (19 Nov, first version). The remote no longer hands its messages to the relay's program. Every AGV with a session announces itself on its PC5 links (Model A discovery); an AGV that loses the gNB picks the strongest announcement above its own Uu signal, sets up a direct link (129 ms median from request to accept) and gets an IPv4 address from the relay, which sends a Remote UE Report up its PDU session. Its app then sends plain IP to the edge; inside the relay AGV the kernel routes it through a GRE tunnel to the relay's nrUE, which NATs it into its session, and the edge's commands come back through the same NAT. On the boards, 15 minutes: 2 of 16,238 uplink messages lost (LiDAR scans on an AGV's own UE, not relayed), 26 direct links, 56 reports; sidelink positioning 189 ms p50, 305 ms p90, commands to a remote 70 ms p50. What is still emulated: the PC5 signalling is UDP over the sidelink IP rather than PC5-S/PC5-RRC, and the SD-RSRP is the model's, not measured.
    • 3GPP procedures for the relay (19 Nov). Four steps closer to TS 23.304 / 24.554 / 38.304: who may relay and who may use a relay follows SIB12-style thresholds (an AGV relays only while its Uu RSRP is between −95 and −31 dBm; a remote selects only below −100 dBm, from relays above −90 dBm SD-RSRP); the link is set up with the PC5-S exchange (request, security mode command and complete with null algorithms, accept with "IP address configuration: DHCPv4 server") and a Model A announcement in the 24.554 shape; the remote gets its address by DHCPv4 from its relay; and every nrUE now has a second PDU session on DNN relay (free5GC, 12.1.2.0/24), into which the relay NATs its remotes' traffic. On the boards, 15 minutes: 1 of 16,260 uplink messages lost (on an AGV's own UE), 21 over 1 s; 23 links, each set up at the first try in 651 ms median (567–777 ms, against 129 ms with the earlier two-message exchange). Delays are 30–45 ms higher on every path, also on an AGV's own UE: most likely the cost of the second PDU session (a second radio bearer to schedule), not yet measured on its own.
    • Secured relay, measured links, QoS, report to the SMF (19 Nov, now the live scenario). PC5 security (keys per link after TS 33.503, MAC on PC5-S, the remote's traffic and DHCP in ESP AES-GCM), SD-RSRP with each board pair's measured deviation, QoS classes per flow (PQI 58/59 → 5QI 7/9, a priority queue on every interface that sends relayed traffic), and the Remote UE Report as NAS to the SMF (patched OAI UE and free5GC SMF; 50 reports, 50 responses). 15 minutes on the boards: none lost through a relay (2 of 16,256 on an AGV's own UE), 25 over 1 s, relayed positioning 209 · 346 ms, link set-up 683 ms: security costs nothing measurable here. A run recorded while the SMF and the UE were compiled on the same PCs had 331 messages over 1 s, nearly all in one minute on one AGV's own UE: the gNB and the UEs run in real time on those PCs, so it was not used.

    Coverage: where the gNB does not reach

    the live scenario since 13 Nov · --topology cover

    The AGVs drive the loop as a platoon. In the shielded cold store the gNB signal falls to −118 dBm; below −100 dBm an AGV switches to the sidelink. More in the Annex ›

    The AGVs drive the same loop as a platoon: A leads and stops at the checkpoints, B and C follow 8 m apart, and an AGV with a session waits for the one behind it when the gap grows past 14 m. Whether an AGV can use the gNB depends on where it is, not on its rank by distance. A log-distance path loss (n = 3.5 among racks) puts the signal at about −80 dBm almost everywhere on the floor; the far corner is a shielded cold store (insulated steel panels), which adds up to 32 dB over the first 3 m past its door line. Inside, the signal falls to −118 dBm. Below −100 dBm an AGV stops sending on its own UE and goes over one sidelink hop through another AGV that still has a session. Since 19 Nov that AGV is a layer-3 UE-to-network relay as in TS 23.304: with thresholds configured as SIB12 would carry them, every AGV whose Uu RSRP is between −95 and −31 dBm and whose relay PDU session is up sends a discovery announcement on its PC5 links every 0.5 s; an AGV below −100 dBm measures each one's SD-RSRP (the same path-loss law, no store walls, since the door is open), keeps only relays above −90 dBm (about 34 m), takes the strongest (2 dB to reselect), sets up the link with PC5-S and gets its address by DHCPv4 from that relay. Once on a relay it keeps it until its own session is up, and stops before the relay's SD-RSRP would fall within 2 dB of −90 dBm. Before, the controller simply picked the nearest AGV with a session, and only one nearer than the gNB; below −110 dBm for 1 s its UE has a radio link failure and is stopped, which really drops its connection to the gNB. Back above −100 dBm for 2 s it attaches again, and keeps using the sidelink until its session has settled (attach 3.7 s median, then 8 s). Only one AGV is on the sidelink at a time: an AGV with a session stops 1.5 m before the dead zone while another AGV is on the sidelink, either because it is that AGV's relay or because going in would need a second sidelink. The AGV on the sidelink never waits: it drives on until it has its own direct connection again (session up and settled), and then the AGV at the door goes in. Coming out of the store, an AGV keeps its sidelink until its own session is up (about 3.7 s): if driving on would take it out of its relay's reach, with the relay no longer nearer than the gNB, it stops 2 m short of that and waits for its session. In practice that is the leader, whose relay waits at the door behind it. As a fallback the app also queues the uplink for up to 5 s while its own UE attaches, and up to 3 s while a direct link is set up.

    Coverage runs: notes on each change · in the Annex ›

    • Spread out, the AGVs a third of a lap apart: every relay is in reach somewhere and almost nothing is lost, but the relay can be across the floor, farther than the gNB, which a real sidelink would not reach.
    • Platoon, relays in reach: only an AGV nearer than the gNB may relay. In a convoy, all three end up in the cold store together, and 8 % of the uplink is lost (1,083 messages), 105 s with no AGV on the gNB at all.
    • Last link holds: the last AGV with a session stops at the door, so the platoon is never cut off, but the leader, out of the store and attaching, is by then farther from the held AGV than the gNB: 80 messages lost, once a lap. Queuing the uplink for up to 5 s while its own UE attaches brings that to 1.
    • One sidelink at a time: only the AGVs behind ever waited, so after a few holds the convoy came apart around the loop and the AGV in the store had no relay in reach: 12 % lost. (With the convoy closed into a ring, every AGV counted as a follower and skipped the checkpoint stops, hence the faster pace.)
    • Platoon kept together: an AGV with a session waits for the one behind, so the AGV at the door is always near enough to relay. 0 of 15,249 uplink messages lost; 2 % arrive after more than 1 s, mostly queued while an AGV leaving the store attaches. The cost is pace: about 22 laps an hour against 29, and the sidelink's p90 rises to about 400 ms.
    • Sidelink kept until the NR link is up: leaving the store, the leader used to drive away from its relay at the door before its own UE had attached, and its uplink waited in the queue. Now an AGV on the sidelink stops while its relay is still 2 m nearer than the gNB and drives on once its session is up: 6 stops in 14 minutes, all AGV A (the leader, whose relay is behind it), about 3 s each. 0 uplink lost, nothing queued, messages over 1 s down from 323 to 77, and the sidelink's p90 back down to 287 ms, at no cost in pace.
    • Layer-3 relay, first version (19 Nov): the same driving, but the relay is now chosen and run as in TS 23.304. The remote picks its relay from discovery announcements (SD-RSRP), sets up a direct link (request to accept: 129 ms median, 26 links) and gets an IP address on it; the relay reports it to the edge (56 Remote UE Reports in 16 minutes) and routes and NATs its packets in the kernel. In 15 minutes: 2 of 16,238 uplink messages lost, both LiDAR scans on an AGV's own UE, none through a relay. The 132 over 1 s came in two bursts around re-attaches, 92 of them from AGVs on their own UE. Sidelink positioning 189 ms p50, 305 ms p90: about 10–20 ms more than the application relay, at the same pace.
    • 3GPP procedures for the relay (19 Nov): who may relay and who may use a relay now follows SIB12-style thresholds (TS 38.331): an AGV announces itself only while its Uu RSRP is between −95 and −31 dBm and its relay PDU session (DNN relay) is up; an AGV selects a relay only below −100 dBm, from announcements above −90 dBm SD-RSRP. The link is set up with the TS 24.554 PC5-S exchange (request, security mode command and complete with null algorithms, accept with "IP address configuration: DHCPv4 server"), and the remote gets its address by DHCPv4 from the relay; the relay reports it with that address. Set-up takes 651 ms (median of 23 links, 567–777 ms) instead of 129 ms: 8 messages over the sidelink instead of 2, none retransmitted, none rejected. In 15 minutes: 1 of 16,260 uplink messages lost (on an AGV's own UE), 21 over 1 s instead of 132, 11 stops to keep a relay in reach instead of 17 (the reach is now the SD-RSRP threshold, about 34 m). Delays are 30–45 ms higher on every path, also on an AGV's own UE (positioning 185 ms p50, against 139): most likely the second PDU session on every UE, not yet checked.
    • Secured relay, measured links, QoS, report to the SMF, the live scenario since 19 Nov: four more steps. PC5 security: keys per link from both sides' nonces (KNRP-sess after TS 33.503, the root generated per run instead of provisioned), every PC5-S message from the security mode command on carries a MAC, and the remote's IP traffic and DHCP go in ESP (AES-GCM-128) over the sidelink. Measured SD-RSRP: each board pair is calibrated on its first 40 beacons and the selection uses the path-loss model plus the link's measured, filtered deviation (−9 to +3.5 dB seen). QoS: positioning PQI 58 → 5QI 7, LiDAR PQI 59 → 5QI 9, carried as DSCP and served by a priority queue on every interface that sends relayed traffic (OAI has one QoS flow per session, so no separate flows on Uu). Remote UE Report over NAS: the relay's UE sends it in UL NAS transport on its relay PDU session; a patched free5GC SMF decodes it, tracks each relay's remote UEs and answers: 50 reports, 50 responses, about 0.1–0.2 s each. In 15 minutes: 2 of 16,256 uplink messages lost, both on an AGV's own UE, none through a relay; 25 over 1 s; link set-up 683 ms median (26 links, 530–932 ms), no MAC failures; relayed positioning 209 · 346 ms, the same as without security. A run between the two (security and measured links, no QoS) gave the same numbers; one recorded while the SMF and the UE were being rebuilt on the same PCs is not used.
    • Sidelink mode 1 (20 Nov), the live scenario: the gNB now schedules the sidelink. Every AGV with its own NR session adds a sidelink buffer status report to its uplink, and the gNB gives it a slice of the next window sized to it; the AGV out of coverage sends in a pool at the end of the window (mode 2). The boards (nrlink v7) hold the staged packets and send only in their slice. The window shrank from 25 ms fixed (10.3 ms of it used) to 16.4 ms on average (grants, guards and the pool), but the NR part of the cycle stayed at about 57 ms, so cycles went only from 12.3 to 13.5 a second. The AGVs on their own NR session did as before (0–0.2 % lost). The remote did not: 9.9 % of its positioning messages and 15.8 % of its LiDAR scans lost, against 0 %, and its positioning took 259 ms instead of 209 (p50). The cause is below the relay: 1.5–2 % of the sidelink UEs' items never arrived (0–0.3 % with the fixed window), so the OAI UEs waited for the missing pieces until they were more than 400 ms late and dropped them. The messages that did arrive cut short (9–10 bytes missing, the same at every receiver) point to bytes lost on the serial line into the sending board, which now receives the packets ahead of its slice while it is busy passing on everything it hears. A test that stops staging while a window is on the air is running; the pool was never full (about 3 of 6–8 ms used).

    IP packet = payload + 28 B; one air packet carries up to 255 B. More in the Annex ›

    IP packet = payload + 28 B (IPv4 + UDP). An air packet carries up to 255 B, so a LiDAR scan crosses each radio hop as 4 packets and a positioning message or a command as 1. Through the layer-3 relay a remote's IP packet crosses the sidelink as it is; inside the relay AGV the GRE tunnel adds 24 B, off the air (the application relay of the dual runs added a 2 B hop header instead). Commands come from the edge every 500 ms to each AGV, to the address and port its last message came from: for a remote, its relay's NAT address, which sends them on. PC5 signalling: announcements from every AGV allowed to relay, four PC5-S messages and four DHCPv4 messages to set up a link and two of each kind to end it, and a Remote UE Report up the relay's relay PDU session for each lease and release.

    Two gateways, one remote

    blue Uu (NR to the gNB) · magenta PC5 (sidelink)
    A remote positioning, step by step (the dual-gateway runs: relayed by the application)

    The remote sends each message through either gateway, its pick per message. More in the Annex ›

    1. The remote AGV's computer makes a positioning message (position, detections, timestamp).
    2. It looks at the last beacons it heard from the two gateways. Each beacon carries the gateway's position, whether its PDU session is up and settled (8 s after the attach), and how many bytes it sent up since its last beacon.
    3. It picks a gateway from them. In the recorded runs the rule is "load": the gateway that sent less, decided again at every beacon, so the remote's traffic alternates between the two. The live scenario uses "sticky": the remote stays on its gateway at least 5 s and moves only when the other one's beacon shows it sent under 60 % as much, so the gateway serving it changes every few seconds at most. A gateway whose session is not settled is not picked; if neither is, it uses the one whose session is up.
    4. The message crosses one PC5 hop to that gateway, over OAI's NR sidelink: the remote's sidelink UE for that pair, inside the gNB's sidelink window, then the gateway's sidelink UE.
    5. The gateway's computer hands it to its own nrUE, which sends it up in its own uplink grants, beside the gateway's own messages.
    6. gNB, UPF, edge server: the edge logs the arrival time and the address it came from.
    7. The edge sends each AGV's commands to the address its last message came from, so a command for the remote comes down through the gateway it last used, which passes it over their PC5 link.

    These runs relay at the application; the coverage scenario relays at layer 3. More in the Annex ›

    In these runs the gateways relay at the application: the gateway's program receives the remote's message and sends it again as its own. The coverage scenario now relays at layer 3 instead (--relay l3, see Packet sequence): the remote sends ordinary IP packets to the edge from an address its relay gave it, and the relay's kernel routes and NATs them into its PDU session without the program seeing them.
