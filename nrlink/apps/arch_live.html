Slot schemes

how each radio shares its time · drawn to scale

nFAPI over nrlink: OAI's TDD pattern, slowed down

The VNF schedules a normal NR TDD pattern: 30 kHz subcarriers, a 5 ms period of 10 slots, 7 downlink (D), 1 special (S: 6 DL symbols, 4 guard, 4 UL) and 2 uplink (U). The PNF does not run on a clock. It waits until gnb_bridge has broadcast the slot and, for S and U slots, polled both UE boards for their uplink. So a DL slot takes about 3.5 ms and an uplink slot about 28 ms, and the period stretches from 5 ms to about 106 ms (94 slots/s, 21× slower than real NR). Half of the polls wait out the full 25 ms timeout, which is most of the uplink slots' time.

Bluetooth LE: connection events on one shared adapter

Each UE board is the central of its own LE connection to the core's Intel adapter. They talk only in connection events, every 45 ms (measured from the LE Enhanced Connection Complete event: interval 45 ms, latency 0, supervision timeout 420 ms). In each event the board sends first and the Intel adapter answers, with up to 27 B of data per packet (no data length extension), about 0.7 ms per exchange. Each event hops to the next of the 37 data channels (2404–2478 MHz). Both links share the Intel radio, so their events interleave. ble_ip_link paces each link to 40 kbit/s, about 225 B, or 9 packets, per event.

The offset between the two links' events is drawn at 20 ms for illustration; the controller chooses it. nrlink's fixed channel, 2402 MHz, is Bluetooth advertising channel 37, not one of the data channels.

What the numbers say
  1. 5G NR path. It carries about 44 kbit/s up and 90 kbit/s down without loss, shared by both nrUEs. Its queues are deep, so a TCP flow pushes the round trip from about 0.5 s to 3–10 s.
  2. Bluetooth path. Each link is shaped to 40 kbit/s on air. After IPsec and GRE overhead, 20–30 kbit/s of UDP gets through cleanly. TCP stays poor: one lost segment at these round trips stalls it for seconds.
  3. Why the n3iwues dropped out. The hci_bb boards talk to the PC over a 115200-baud UART with no flow control. Under load a board stops answering HCI (command tx timeout) while its radio link stays up, and nothing gets through. The n3iwue's IKE then gives up and the container exits. This happened 2 times in the final 10-minute run, and 4 times earlier in the day.
  4. What changed on the link. ble_ip_link.py now paces traffic to 40 kbit/s itself. Small packets (pings, ACKs, IKE) go before bulk data, and the bulk queue keeps at most 1 s. That cut the Bluetooth RTT under load from 2–5 s to 0.4–1.1 s. ble_watchdog.sh resets a hung board and restarts its n3iwue; stress.py waits for the session and lists each recovery in its results.
  5. The real fix. Wire each BLE board's UART to a USB-serial adapter with RTS/CTS, or use ordinary USB Bluetooth adapters on the UE PC.

When a board hangs, its main CPU goes completely silent: no HCI events, not even answers to vendor commands it handles itself (0xFDB0). The radio core keeps the LE link up, so the core still sees the connection. One link hangs as often as two.

Ruled out, and what is left
  1. Ruled out by test: the UART overrun flag (the NXP driver clears it), the 128 B receive ring, failed asserts and faults, a stalled main loop, unread UART input, interrupts masked in the main loop, and stack overflow.
  2. Most likely now: an interrupt handler that never returns, at the same top priority as the tick, so nothing can preempt it to report. The candidates are the UART and the radio-core message (RPMsg) handlers.
  3. Next, cheapest first: a release build (all of this is NXP's -O0 debug build, and an optimised build changes timing a lot); a diagnostic build that puts every other interrupt below the tick; then a debugger session, which needs the MCXW727 device pack (pyocd's generic target gets WAIT on every board, healthy ones too).
  4. Meanwhile: ble_watchdog.sh resets a hung board in about 10 s and restarts its n3iwue, and ble_ip_link.py now keeps each tun across reconnects, so IPsec survives a radio dropout.
