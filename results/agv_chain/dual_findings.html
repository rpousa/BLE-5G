• Two gateways carry L2 where the chain could not. With the usual 25 ms windows every flow lost 3.4 % or less (the chain: 9 % at the pivot, 38 % at the relay, 57 % at the remote), and a command reached the remote in 134 ms with 1.6 % lost (the chain: 458 ms, 51 %). Each sidelink hop lost 0.8 % or less.
  • Why: far less sidelink traffic. The middle AGV no longer hops at all, and the remote hops once instead of twice. At L2 the boards carried about 14 hops a second and used 15 ms of each 25 ms window; the chain needed more than its windows held (21 ms in use, and the rest aged out). The sidelink items left room for the position beacons too.
  • The uplink is now the limit. The two UEs together sent about 7.0 kB/s (56 kbit/s) up, close to the 62–65 kbit/s these boards carry on Uu. That is where the 0.5–0.9 s pose delays at L2 come from: messages wait in the UEs for grants, but they are not lost.
  • Wider windows make it worse. At 35 and 50 ms the gNB runs fewer frames (8.5 and 7.8 a second instead of 9.8), so fewer uplink slots, and the uplink no longer keeps up: poses waited 5–9 s and 4–38 % aged out, while the sidelink, now with room to spare, lost almost nothing. For two gateways the narrow window is right; the chain needed wider windows only because of its double hops.
  • The remote's choice matters. Sending all its traffic through gateway 1 (at 25 ms) made the poses wait 2.5–3.4 s (p50) and the remote lose 6–7 %: gateway 1's UE queued while gateway 2's had room. Picking the gateway whose last beacon said it sent less split the remote's traffic about evenly and kept delays at 0.5–0.9 s. At 35 ms, where the uplink is overloaded either way, always-gateway-1 was still worse (15–22 s against 5–7 s).
  • L1: every flow lost 0.9 % or less. The remote's pose took 205 ms (the chain: 330 ms over two hops), and gateway 2's own pose 157 ms (the chain's relay: 215 ms).
  • About a third of the remote's messages went up its own UE. After a role change the old gateway, now the remote, keeps its session until the new gateway's UE has attached and settled (about 5 s plus 8 s), and while it has one it sends directly. With 3–4 role changes a minute that is a third of the time.
  • What is simulated is as on the relay chain page: the AGVs' motion, the application data, coverage (a UE out of coverage is stopped), and the PHY (GFSK packets carry the slots' contents). The gNB, the UEs, the sidelink stack, the core and every delay and loss here are real. The gateways' beacons are the controller's own packets; what the remote reads from them is the gateway's session state and the bytes it sent since its last beacon.
  • Live on the testbed. From 12 to 13 Nov the dual gateway was the testbed's live scenario (nrlink.sh live start --scenario dual on both PCs, the core PC first): L1, 25 ms windows, the remote on the sticky rule. The boards' LEDs show who carries what: red the remote, blue the gateway the remote sends through (NR and sidelink), green the other gateway (NR only). The core PC's live page (port 8090, on the LAN) shows this page with a live card on top: each board with the colour its LED shows, the AGVs moving in the same colours with both gateways' Uu links and the remote's sidelink to the blue gateway, every message's delay and the last minute's table. The sticky rule has not been through the L2 window tests; the recorded runs used the load rule.
  • Coverage by position (13 Nov, the live scenario). With the gNB's signal set by where each AGV is, every AGV lost its connection in the cold store once a lap: 18 radio link failures in 13 minutes, each followed by an attach that worked (median 3.7 s), and no message dropped for want of a session. The sidelink carried 26 % of all uplink messages, 25–26 % for each AGV. Over it, positioning took 189 ms at p50 against 128–140 ms on an AGV's own UE, LiDAR 325 against 220 ms, commands 90 against 63–66 ms, with at most 0.2 % lost. In that run the sticky pick moved the AGV between the two gateways every 5 s, because the one carrying it always looked busier; since then the live scenario picks the nearest gateway (keeping the current one while it is within 2 m of the nearest), which changes about twice per outage: once at the start and about once as the AGVs move.
  • Platoon, relays in reach, holding before the dead zone (13 Nov, briefly the live scenario). The AGVs drive as a convoy 8 m apart, and an AGV out of coverage relays only through the nearest AGV with a session that is nearer to it than the gNB. When only one AGV still has a settled session it stops 1.5 m short of the cold store until another has one again: 6 holds of 20 s in 13 minutes, and never a moment with all three AGVs cut off. 21 attaches, all up (median 3.5 s). The first try without a queue lost 2.2 % of the sidelink messages: the leader leaves the store while its UE is still attaching, and by then the held AGV is farther from it than the gNB, so it has no relay. With the app queuing for up to 5 s, 1 uplink message of 14,140 was lost and 49 arrived after more than 1 s. Over the sidelink: positioning 187 ms p50, LiDAR 308 ms, commands 75 ms; on an AGV's own UE 123–145, 220 and 62–66 ms.
  • No lost packets: one sidelink at a time, the platoon kept together (13 Nov, the live scenario until 16 Nov). An AGV with a session stops at the cold store's door only while another AGV is on the sidelink (as its relay, or because it would need a second sidelink); the AGV on the sidelink drives on to its own direct connection; and an AGV with a session waits for the one behind it, so the convoy stays within reach. In 14 minutes on the boards: 0 of 15,249 uplink messages lost, never two AGVs off the gNB, 11 holds (median 25 s), 2 % of messages over 1 s. Without keeping the convoy together the same rule lost 12 %, as the AGVs spread around the loop out of sidelink reach. The comparison card has every step.
  • The sidelink is kept until the NR link is up (16 Nov, the live scenario until 19 Nov). Leaving the cold store, AGV A (the leader) used to drive out of its relay's reach before its own UE had attached; the simulation puts that at 24–48 s of every 30 minutes with neither a session nor a relay, covered only by the 5 s uplink queue. Now an AGV on the sidelink keeps its relay until its own session is up, and stops before it would drive out of that relay's reach. On the boards: 6 stops in 14 minutes, all AGV A, about 3 s each; 0 uplink messages lost and none queued; sidelink positioning 180 ms p50, 287 ms p90.
  • A layer-3 relay, as in 3GPP (19 Nov, now the live scenario). The remote no longer hands its messages to the relay's program. Every AGV with a session announces itself on its PC5 links (Model A discovery); an AGV that loses the gNB picks the strongest announcement above its own Uu signal, sets up a direct link (129 ms median from request to accept) and gets an IPv4 address from the relay, which sends a Remote UE Report up its PDU session. Its app then sends plain IP to the edge; inside the relay AGV the kernel routes it through a GRE tunnel to the relay's nrUE, which NATs it into its session, and the edge's commands come back through the same NAT. On the boards, 15 minutes: 2 of 16,238 uplink messages lost (LiDAR scans on an AGV's own UE, not relayed), 26 direct links, 56 reports; sidelink positioning 189 ms p50, 305 ms p90, commands to a remote 70 ms p50. What is still emulated: the PC5 signalling is UDP over the sidelink IP rather than PC5-S/PC5-RRC, and the SD-RSRP is the model's, not measured.
